# CLAUDE.md

Classic Arkanoid (Breakout) game. Unity `6000.5.6f1`, Universal Render Pipeline, new Input System.

Gameplay is strictly 2D (2D physics on the XY plane) but the visuals are 3D: bricks and paddle are box meshes, the ball is a sphere, all with URP Lit materials (`Assets/Materials`), lit by the scene's directional light in front of a shadow-catching `Backdrop` plane at z 0.6. The light faces the playfield head-on horizontally and tilts down by `ArkanoidSetup.LightPitch` (30°). Nothing here casts onto a floor — every shadow lands on a surface *behind* its caster — so the shadow's offset from the object is `gap × tan(pitch)`: a steeper, "higher" light smears the shadow further down the backdrop, and a shallower one tucks it back in behind the object. 30° keeps shadows readable as a depth cue without the long drop the earlier 50° gave. The camera is perspective (FOV 50 at z −13.5, framing-equivalent to the old orthographic size 6). Bricks are tinted per instance via `MaterialPropertyBlock` (`Brick.SetColor`), and the crack overlay stays a `SpriteRenderer` child sitting just in front of the box's face. A generated tileable brick-masonry texture (`Assets/Textures/BrickWall.png`, grayscale so the material tint colors it) survives from the walled playfield in the menu's block lettering; the `WallSide`/`WallTop` materials and meshes it was made for do not stand in the scene any more.

## The playfield is the frame

**A round runs the whole width of the window**, exactly as the menu screens always have: there are no walls standing in the picture, only three invisible `Border`s laid along the left, right and top edges of what the camera actually sees, so the ball ricochets off the edge of the screen in a burst of sparks. Nothing about this can be authored — a frame is only known once there is a window, and it changes when the window does — so `Playfield` (on the room's root, which `GameManager.playfieldObjects` switches off for the menu) fits it all in `OnEnable` and again whenever the window is resized: the borders, the `Backdrop`'s size (measured at its *own* z, since it stands further back than the room and has a wider frame to cover, plus `BackdropOverhang` so its edge is never seen), and how far the paddle may travel (`Paddle.FitTo`, which keeps the paddle's measured body inside the frame). `GameManager.BuildLevel` reads `Playfield.HalfWidth` back and cuts the brick grid to as many whole slots as fit with `BrickEdgeMargin` (0.9) clear either side — 12 columns at 16:9 where the walled field held 8. Only the width is worked out that way: the camera's field of view is vertical, so the frame is the same height whatever the window's shape and the rows stay at the `y0` 4.6 they were authored at. The frame is measured as extents and centred on *the room* rather than on the camera, since the view travels here from the menu with both rooms standing.

The score and lives readouts moved to the **bottom** corners with the widening: the field reaches the top corners now, and the strip under the paddle is the one part of the screen a round has nothing standing in.

The menu's paddle and ball are scaled by `ArkanoidSetup.MenuPlayScale` — the ratio of the two planes' depths from the camera (9.8 / 13.5) — because the menu's plane stands nearer the camera and the same world size looked bigger there. `MenuBallSpeed` is scaled by the same ratio, so the menu's ball still crosses its screen at the gentler rate it was given rather than merely being slower in world units. `Ball.RestOffset` measures the paddle it sits on and its own radius rather than using a fixed height, so the smaller pair doesn't float apart.

The paddle has rounded corners: a rounded-rectangle prism mesh (`Assets/Meshes/Paddle.asset`, 2 × 0.4 × 0.5, corner radius 0.15, authored at final size so the transform is unit-scale) paired with a `BoxCollider2D` shrunk by the radius on every side (1.7 × 0.1) plus `edgeRadius: 0.15` — exactly the same rounded rectangle — so the ball's bounce reflects off the corner curve's true normal. `Ball.cs` applies its arcade paddle-bounce override only when the contact normal is straight up (flat-top hits); corner hits keep the engine's reflection.

There are four brick shapes, and each prefab's 2D collider matches its visual outline exactly so the ball reflects off the shape's true contact normal:

- `Brick.prefab` — the normal 1.5 × 0.5 box (scaled stock cube + `BoxCollider2D`).
- `HalfBrick.prefab` — a half-width 0.68 × 0.5 box; two of them plus the level's 0.14 gap fill one normal slot (2 × 0.68 + 0.14 = 1.5).
- `RoundedBrick.prefab` — full-size with corner radius 0.12: a rounded-rectangle prism mesh (`Assets/Meshes/BrickRounded.asset`, built by the same `BuildRoundedPrismMesh` as the paddle, unit-scale transform) with a 1.26 × 0.26 `BoxCollider2D` + `edgeRadius: 0.12`, so corner hits reflect off the curve.
- `RoundBrick.prefab` — a 0.5-diameter sphere (stock sphere at scale 0.5) with a `CircleCollider2D` (radius 0.5 × scale = 0.25 world), so hits deflect along the circle's radial normal. Its crack overlay child is scaled 0.7 (≈ inscribed square) to stay over the circular silhouette.

`GameManager.rowKinds` assigns a shape per row (currently Rounded / Normal / Half / Round / Normal, top to bottom); Half and Round rows spawn two bricks per slot at slot center ± 0.41 and half the row's points each. `PrefabFor` falls back to the normal brick if a variant prefab is unwired. All four prefabs share the white `Brick.mat` tinted per instance, the crack overlay child, and the same `Brick` component — hardness and points are set per spawn.

## The main menu screen

The menu is **a room of its own**, a screen's width (`MainMenuPanel.ScreenSpacing`) to the left of the playfield in world X — it does not stand in front of the playfield any more. That is what gives START something to travel across: `GameManager.TravelToPlayfield` builds the round first and then slides the camera right from the menu's room to the playfield's over `TravelDuration` (0.6 s), switching the menu off and raising the HUD on arrival, in the same frame the camera lands so the menu's backdrop is never seen over the round. Nothing but the camera moves. `traveling` holds off every key on the way (the ball is already sitting on the paddle), and `MenuBackdropWidth` (24) is enough for the menu's backdrop to fill its own room's frame at any sane aspect ratio. It may overhang the playfield's room freely — the menu is switched off in the same frame the view arrives there, so the only time this backdrop is seen from outside its own room is on the journey between the two, which is exactly where it should be. Coming *back* — ESC, or an end screen — is a cut rather than a journey: `ShowMenu` puts the camera over the menu in the same frame the opaque menu comes up, so there is nothing to see. Because the rooms are apart in X, `Paddle` clamps to `xLimit` either side of **where it was authored** (`homeX`, taken in `Awake`) rather than either side of x 0, which would drag the menu's paddle out of its own room.

The menu is not a UI panel at all — it is a small playable scene, and there is no UGUI in it. It is also **two screens side by side**: the title board, and the hall of fame a screen's width (`MainMenuPanel.ScreenSpacing`, 20 units) to its left. Both hang off `MenuSlider`, and picking the hall of fame slides that group right rather than cutting to another view. **Every arrow scrolls the way it points**, which is what settles the layout: the left-pointing HALL OF FAME arrow travels left to reach the hall, so the hall is on the left, and the right-pointing arrow on the hall travels right to come back — as the right-pointing START does to leave the menu for a round. The champion arrows follow the same rule (see below). The `MenuScreen` root (authored inactive, carrying `MainMenuPanel` itself) holds:

- `MenuBackdrop` — an opaque box at z −3, `MenuBackdropWidth` (24) across, covering the menu room's frame on any sane aspect ratio. Outside the slider, so it stays put while the screens travel across it.
- `MenuSlider/MenuBoard` — `MenuTitle`, the word ARKANOID, tilted 8° so its tops and sides catch the light and it throws a shadow onto the backdrop. It is **eight children, one brick per letter**, each with a `MenuTitleBlock` and a `BoxCollider2D` covering the glyph's whole 5 × 7 box (holes included — a collider tracing the strokes would let the ball rattle around inside an O). A hit shatters the letter into `Debris` and switches it off; `MainMenuPanel` switches them all back on when the menu opens and whenever this board is the one travelling into the frame, so breaking the word is a toy rather than a permanent edit. Beside it stand the two options: **START is a right-pointing arrow banner to the right of the word, HALL OF FAME a left-pointing one to its left**, at ±`MenuArrowX` (6.45), which leaves about 0.6 of a unit both between an arrow and the word and between an arrow and the frame's edge.
- `MenuSlider/MenuHall` — the hall of fame (see below).
- `MenuPlay` — the part the player drives: a `MenuPaddle` reusing the playfield paddle's mesh, collider and `Paddle` script, and a `MenuBall` instance of the ball prefab at a gentler speed (6.5 vs the playfield's 8). Outside the slider too, so the paddle and ball stay where they are while the world behind them moves.

Everything hittable sits on the `MenuPlaneZ` −3.7 plane.

**Picking an option means hitting its arrow with the ball**, and the arrow breaks under it: the hit shatters it into `Debris` in its own colour and switches it off, and `MainMenuPanel` holds the choice back for `ShatterPause` (0.9 s) before acting on it, so the pieces visibly fall before the screen changes rather than the hit cutting away instantly. `OnOptionHit` refuses every hit after the first, which is what stops the still-live ball from picking a second option during the pause, and refuses an arrow belonging to the screen that isn't up, since both screens are in the scene at once and only the slide separates them. **A refused arrow doesn't shatter** — it bounces the ball like the frame does, and `OnOptionHit`'s `bool` answer is what keeps it standing. That isn't cosmetic: an arrow is a screen's only way out, and the only mending it gets is on the way *in* to its board (below), so an arrow broken on the board already in the frame would stay broken. Shattering refused hits was tried, on the theory that an arrow merely bouncing the ball reads as an impact the game failed to notice, and it stranded the player: knock out HALL OF FAME with a stray bounce during a change and the title screen has no way to reach the hall at all. The lettering is the toy; the arrows are the controls. Every arrow is a banner — a body drawn out to a point at one end, corners rounded — carrying its label (START, HALL OF / FAME, NEXT, MENU) standing proud of its front face. The banner is sized for the gaps around it first and the lettering follows, which is why `MenuArrowLabelCell` is as small as it is. The label is centred on the *body* rather than the whole shape, so the point stays empty and the letters don't run out past the slants. The `PolygonCollider2D` is the arrow's own rounded outline, so a hit anywhere reflects off the shape the player can see, corners included. Green means "on you go" (START, and the way back from the hall), blue means "the other way" (HALL OF FAME, and the next champion). Debris fragments are unparented root objects that outlive their spawner, so `Hide` calls `Debris.ClearAll()` — otherwise the menu's rubble would go on raining over the first seconds of the round.

Only `StartGame` is ever raised to `GameManager` as `OptionChosen`; the hall of fame and the way back are the menu's own business, carried out by `CarryOut` — slide, or advance the champion — after which the shattered arrow is put back and nothing else is (the board being travelled to was mended before it set off; whatever the ball broke on the way stays broken — see below).

**The rally carries on across a screen change**: the ball is not caught and re-served when the menu moves between its screens, because the paddle and the ball are the one thing on the menu that isn't a screen, and stopping them for one made the menu feel like a series of pages rather than one game being played. The ball stays solid throughout, and hitting whatever is passing in front of it is part of that: it was tried the other way — the ball made "transparent" to both boards from the moment a choice was made until its next bounce, so nothing could be knocked apart mid-change — and it played worse than leaving the ball alone, whether the transparency ended at the next bounce or was held to the end of the change. Do not reach for it again without a clearer reason than tidiness. `busy` no longer holds off the rest of `Update` either: the ball still has to be caught when it falls out of the frame and served again when it does, and all `busy` means now is that a second option can't be picked (`OnOptionHit`).

There is no keyboard or mouse path to the options. The ball waits on the paddle until SPACE, exactly like a round — `GameManager.OnGUI` draws the same "press SPACE to launch" prompt (lower on screen for the menu, where mid-screen is where the options are). The paddle rests under the middle, between the two arrows, so a ball launched straight up picks nothing and the player has to aim.

**The menu's field is the frame itself**, as a round's is. `GameManager.playfieldObjects` (the `Playfield` root and the playfield `Paddle`) is switched off for the whole `Menu` state and back on in `NewGame`: 2D physics ignores Z, so the playfield's colliders would otherwise fence in the menu's ball — the rooms are apart in X, but a ball driven out of the menu's room would still find them — and both paddles would answer the arrow keys at once. In their place the menu builds three walls of its own (`Border`, shared with the playfield) and lays them along the left, right and top edges of what the camera actually sees, inner faces exactly on the frame, so the ball **ricochets off the edge of the screen**. They are built at runtime by `MainMenuPanel.FitToFrame` rather than authored, for the same reason the option arrows are pulled in there: the frame is only known once there is a window, and it changes when the window does. They carry no renderer — the border is the screen's edge, not an object — so what says the ball hit something is the `Ricochet` burst spawned at the point of contact. The frame is measured as extents and then centred on the *room* rather than on the camera, since the view travels out of this room to the playfield with the menu still up; the paddle's travel comes off the same measurement. The bottom is left open: a ball that falls past the paddle materialises back on it, and `OutOfFrame` still tests all four sides as the way home for a ball a resized frame left on the wrong side of a wall. `Paddle.cs` keeps its own Z rather than zeroing it, so the menu's paddle stays on the menu plane.

### The hall of fame

`MenuHall` shows **one champion at a time**: their name over their score, in the same block letters as the title, with a left arrow for the next champion and a right arrow back to the menu — the arrow the player came in on, mirrored. The names come from the record book, so both lines are built at runtime by `HallOfFame` rather than authored; the meshes are its own and are destroyed as it moves on. The list is `RecordBook.Load()` sorted by score, best first, capped at `MaxChampions` (10), and it wraps — the last champion's next is the first again. The champion does not change on the spot: `HallOfFame.Advance` is a coroutine that builds the next champion a screen's width to the left of the plaque and scrolls both lines right over `ScrollDuration` (0.6 s), so the champion being left behind travels out of the frame the way the left-pointing arrow that was hit points, and the plaque is then put back at its authored X with the arriving champion standing on it. **The arrow that was hit travels in with them**: it shattered to make the choice, and switching it back on where it stood while the name and the score flowed in from the left made it the one thing on the screen appearing out of thin air. `Advance` reads its rest X off the arrow before sending it out to the left (that X is `MainMenuPanel`'s fit to the frame, so it can't be written down here), then carries it along the same travel as the lines. `MainMenuPanel.RestoreOption` still runs afterwards and finds it already standing. The symbols' UV origins stay their place in the word whatever offset they are built at, so the masonry reads the same wherever along the travel the plaque was made. Only real records are shown; an empty book reads NO RECORDS YET, and the "next" arrow is hidden whenever there is nothing to advance to. Both lines share one cell size, taken from the longer of the two against the `MaxWidth` (9) units the plaque has and capped at `CellCap`. **The cap is the gap between the two lines, not a number written down**: a glyph is `GlyphHeight` cells tall and is drawn centred on its line, so the pair only fits while a glyph plus `LineGapCells` (1, the same gap `BlockText` leaves between letters along a line) covers the distance between them, and `CellCap` measures that distance off the authored transforms rather than assuming it. A short name is held back by the cap rather than by the width — "AGX" over "300" has width to spare, and the old fixed `MaxCell` of 0.25 blew it up to 1.75 units a line across the 1.5 the lines stand apart, so the name's feet stood in the top of the score. At the authored 1.5 the cap works out at 0.1875, just under the title's own `MenuTitleCell` (0.2), which is right: the title should stay the largest lettering on the menu. Move a line in the scene and the lettering resizes to match.

**Every symbol of both lines is its own hittable block**, exactly like a letter of the title: one `GameObject` per glyph under the line, carrying its own mesh, a `BoxCollider2D` over the glyph's whole 5 × 7 box and the same `MenuTitleBlock` the title's letters use, so a name and a score can be knocked apart while they are read. A glyph with nothing solid in it (a space) is skipped — nothing to hit and nothing to show. The line object itself holds no mesh any more; it is the anchor, and its `MeshRenderer` is where the symbols take their material from. The symbols are tracked in a list rather than counted off the line's children, because `Destroy` only takes effect at the end of the frame and the children would still be last champion's. Moving on to the next champion rebuilds the plaque outright, and a champion arrived at with half their name missing is a record that can't be read.

**A board is mended on the way in, and never once it is in the frame** (`MainMenuPanel.RestoreBoard`, lettering and the arrows standing beside it together). The rule falls out of the ball playing on through a change: the boards move with the ball still live, so the player watches the hits it lands on them, and putting those letters back the instant the movement stops reads as the game undoing hits it had already shown — which is exactly how it looked when `CarryOut` ended by mending both boards wholesale. So what the ball breaks *during* a change stays broken, and the board carries that damage until it is next the board arriving, which is the one moment nobody can see it being mended. The only thing put back at the end of a change is the arrow that carried it out (`RestoreOption`) — it shattered to make the choice, and without it the board has one fewer way out than it was built with. Reopening the menu still mends everything (`Show`), and the title is no longer the exception that stayed broken until it did.

The letter meshes come from `BlockText`, which lives in the **runtime** assembly precisely because of this screen — a champion's name is only known while the game is running, so the editor setup script and the hall of fame have to share one builder. It holds the 5 × 7 block font (the whole alphabet and the digits, since a name is whatever the player typed, with anything else falling back to `?`) and `BuildMesh`, which makes one box per horizontal run of solid cells in a glyph row rather than one per cell. UVs are taken from each corner's position plus a `uvOrigin` giving the piece's place in the word (× `MenuTitleUvScale` 2), which is what lets the title be eight independently-built letters whose `BrickWall.png` masonry still runs continuously across the joins. The 47-cell word spans 9.4 units at `MenuTitleCell` 0.2, with the arrows filling the rest of the frame out to its edges — the layout is the whole width of a 16:9 screen rather than the 4:3 box that used to hold it in and kept the word small. A narrower screen is handled at runtime instead of on paper: `MainMenuPanel.FitOptionsToFrame` pulls each arrow in from the frame edge it would otherwise hang over, keeping the same gap the 16:9 layout leaves, re-running whenever the window changes size, so the 16:9 layout is the widest the arrows are ever placed rather than the only one that works. `ArrowOutline` gives the arrow banner's shape — corners rounded by `RoundedOutline`, which replaces each corner of a counter-clockwise polygon with an arc tangent to both its edges and trims the radius where an edge is too short (the point is a sharp angle that would otherwise swallow half the shape) — and `BuildArrowMesh` extrudes exactly that, working out each face's winding from the direction it should look rather than hand-winding the two mirrored arrows separately. `BuildLinesMesh` stacks a label too long for one line, which is how HALL OF FAME fits across a banner.

## Layout

- `Assets/Scripts/Paddle.cs` — keyboard paddle movement (`Keyboard.current`, arrows/A-D), clamped to `xLimit` either side of where the paddle was authored (`homeX`, taken in `Awake`) rather than either side of x 0, since the menu's paddle lives in the menu's room a screen's width to the left. `xLimit` itself is handed over by the room the paddle belongs to (`FitTo`) once it has measured its frame — both fields are the window's width now — and the paddle's own body is measured off its renderer, since the menu's paddle is a scaled-down copy of the round's.
- `Assets/Scripts/Ball.cs` — constant-speed ball with a `Damage` value (currently 1) read by bricks on impact. Kinematic and paddle-following while attached; dynamic after launch. **The first launch is a fixed 15° off vertical, to the right** (`LaunchAngle`), and the waiting ball sits at the point on the paddle that angle comes off rather than dead centre: the paddle's arcade bounce turns a hit's distance from the middle, as a fraction of the paddle's half-width, into the tangent of the angle the ball leaves at, so the rest offset is `tan(LaunchAngle) x` the paddle's measured half-width. The paddle is measured rather than assumed, so the menu's ball and the round's both serve the same way — as is the rest *height*, off the paddle's and the ball's own drawn half-heights plus `RestClearance`, since the menu's pair are a scaled-down copy and a fixed height would leave them floating apart. **Across** the paddle is measured by its *collider*, because the bounce measures the same thing and the two have to agree; **upward** by its *renderer*, because `Collider2D.bounds` leaves out `edgeRadius` and the paddle carries most of its height in exactly that, so a collider-measured height sits the ball a corner radius inside the paddle it is meant to rest on. `FixedUpdate` renormalizes velocity to `speed`, and **a ball that has been stopped dead is sent off again** rather than left alone (`PinnedSpeed`, aimed by `Steepen` at `StallEscapeAngle` away from the last surface touched): normalising a heading of nothing gives nothing back, so the bail-out this replaces made a pinned ball a permanently pinned one. Two faces meeting almost head on can stop it — an option arrow's notch, where its tail meets its body, is where it was seen, once refused arrows stopped shattering out from under the ball. Paddle bounces off the flat top override the physics reflection with an angle based on where the ball hit the paddle; hits on the paddle's rounded corners (tilted contact normal) keep the engine's reflection off the curve. The ball knows nothing about the menu's screens changing around it: it is solid to everything, always (see "The main menu screen" for the transparency that was tried here and taken back out).

  **The ball must never settle into a rally it cannot get out of**, which the full-width field made much easier to fall into — three guards, one per way it happens. `MinAngle` (20°) is the floor under every fixed step: it used to be a hair under 9°, which stopped the ball going *exactly* sideways but little else, since a rally that flat crossed the frame in three seconds while climbing a quarter of it and read as a stuck ball; at 20° a crossing gains most of the frame's height. The paddle's own bounce never comes off flatter than 45°, so the floor only ever catches a ball the walls and bricks have flattened. `escapeY` is which way that floor sends a flattened ball, and it is taken from **the last surface the ball touched** rather than being fixed: the old guard defaulted a zero vertical component to *upwards*, so a ball that flattened while lying against the top border was shoved back into it every step and skimmed along it for ever — the endless horizontal rally. `Steepen` always reads `escapeY` rather than the sign of what is left of the ball's own vertical component, because a reflection can leave that sign a hair either side of zero, and away from the last surface is always right. Finally `StallBounces` (3) counts consecutive bounces off *vertical* faces — a side border, or the side of a brick, which return the ball with its vertical component untouched — and aims the ball out at `StallEscapeAngle` (35°) on reaching that count; anything else struck is progress and starts the count again. That last one is a backstop: at `MinAngle` the ball crosses the field at most twice before meeting the top or the paddle, so reaching three means something is flattening it that we did not foresee.
- `Assets/Scripts/Brick.cs` — accumulates the ball's `Damage` per hit against its `Hardness` (1–3, set per row by `GameManager`); destroys itself and reports its `Points` to `GameManager` once damage reaches hardness, spawning falling `Debris` fragments in its color as it goes. While still alive it shows a crack overlay on the prefab's `Cracks` child renderer — light crack up to half damage, heavy crack beyond — from parallel arrays of sprite variants wired in by the editor setup script. On its first hit each brick picks a random variant index plus random X/Y renderer flips, then keeps them, so cracks differ between bricks while escalating damage reads as the same crack spreading (each heavy sprite extends its light counterpart).
- `Assets/Scripts/Debris.cs` — cosmetic rubble for a shattering brick, spawned by `Brick` at destruction (runtime-only, nothing to author in the editor). 6–9 small cube fragments share the brick's material, tinted with per-fragment brightness variation of the brick's color via `MaterialPropertyBlock`; the cube mesh is lifted once off a throwaway `CreatePrimitive` template. Fragments carry no physics components — they are moved by hand in `Update` (initial outward/upward pop, constant gravity, random tumble), so they can never disturb the 2D gameplay colliders; they shrink away over the last third of a 1.2–2 s life (the opaque Lit material can't alpha-fade) and also die below the playfield. `Spawn` takes an optional `amount` multiplier on the fragment count for casters bigger than a brick, and `ClearAll` sweeps every fragment still in the air, for screen changes that would otherwise inherit the last screen's rubble.
- `Assets/Scripts/GameManager.cs` — state machine (Menu/Ready/Playing/EnteringName/GameOver/Won), score, lives, high score persisted via `PlayerPrefs` (`Arkanoid.HighScore`), runtime brick-grid construction (per-row color and hardness: red rows take 3 hits, blue rows 1). The high score is still tracked and persisted, but it is no longer displayed anywhere — it only decides whether a finished round earns a `RecordBook` entry, and the hall of fame is where the numbers are read. The HUD is hidden on the menu and shows score and lives during a round. When a round ends above the record held at round start, switches to `EnteringName`: reads the player's name key by key off `Keyboard.current` in `Update` (backspace edits, ENTER submits, 12-char cap) and appends a `RecordBook` entry. It used to subscribe to `Keyboard.onTextInput`, which never delivered a character and left the field looking dead. Reading keys is also the right path for what the name is *for*: `BlockText`'s 5 × 7 font, which the hall of fame draws the name in, holds only the capitals and the digits, so a champion who typed "Alex" went up on the plaque as "A???" — only glyphs that font can draw can be typed now (A–Z, 0–9, and a space that may not lead). Pushes values into the `ScoreBoard` and drives the `RecordsPanel` and `MainMenuPanel`, and switches `playfieldObjects` (the round's room and its paddle) off for the menu and back on for a round. OnGUI is used for the "press SPACE to launch" banner, which serves the menu's ball as well as a round's, and for the quit confirmation. **ESC leaves a round for the menu, on confirmation**: the prompt sets `Time.timeScale` to 0 so a ball in flight can't be lost while the player reads the question, and swallows every key but its own (Y/ENTER leaves, N/ESC keeps playing). It is only offered from `Ready` and `Playing` — the menu has nothing to leave, name entry is one ENTER from being submitted, and an end screen just goes back on ESC as it does on SPACE. Abandoning a round winds the stored high score back to `previousRecord`, because `SetScore` has been pushing the running score into it all round and a walked-out-on score must not raise the bar the next round has to beat. `ShowMenu` now calls `ClearRound` (ball, brick grid, the round's rubble and ricochet sparks, any name-entry subscription): 2D physics ignores Z, so a ball or a grid of bricks left standing behind the menu's backdrop would still be in the menu ball's way, unseen — which is the same reason `playfieldObjects` goes off. Freezing time is not enough on its own for the components that read the keyboard themselves, so `MainMenuPanel.Update` bails out on `GameManager.Paused` rather than launching the menu's ball behind the prompt; the paddle needs no such guard because it moves by `Time.deltaTime`. Boots into the `Menu` state and reacts to `MainMenuPanel.OptionChosen`, which only ever means "start a game" — the menu's hall of fame is a screen inside the menu now, and nothing here is involved in it; end screens go back to the menu on SPACE or ESC. Every state transition stamps `transitionFrame` and `Update` returns early on that frame, because `MainMenuPanel` reads the same keyboard in the same frame in an undefined order — without it the ENTER that picks `START` would also launch the ball. Serialized references are wired by the editor setup script.
- `Assets/Scripts/ScoreBoard.cs` — display-only UGUI HUD component on the `ScoreBoard` canvas (Score / Lives), anchored to the screen's bottom corners since the field runs the full width. The readouts sit under a `Hud` child, so `SetVisible` can switch the whole HUD off for the menu screen without touching the records and main menu panels on the same canvas; `GameManager` pushes the values in and shows the HUD for a round. There is no high score readout.
- `Assets/Scripts/RecordBook.cs` — static store for the chronological record-holder list (date/name/score), persisted in `PlayerPrefs` (`Arkanoid.Records`) as JSON. `Clear` is for testing and nothing in the game calls it.
- `Assets/Editor/RecordsMenu.cs` — the **Arkanoid ▸ Clear Records** menu command, which empties the record book *and* deletes the stored high score. Both, because the high score is the bar a round has to clear to be asked for a name: an emptied book with the old bar still standing would never reach name entry to be tested.
- `Assets/Scripts/RecordsPanel.cs` — display-only end-of-round overlay under the `ScoreBoard` canvas: name entry after a new record, then the hall-of-fame list. Authored inactive; `GameManager` shows/hides it. It serves the end of a round only — the menu's own hall of fame is scene content (`HallOfFame`), not this panel.
- `Assets/Scripts/MainMenuPanel.cs` — the title screen, which is scene content rather than UI (see "The main menu screen" above). The component sits on the `MenuScreen` root, so `Show`/`Hide` is just switching that subtree on and off. It reassembles the title and every option arrow, fits the arrows, the paddle's travel and the room's three `Border` walls to the frame the camera actually has (`FitToFrame`, re-run whenever the window changes size), parks the paddle back under the middle and re-attaches the ball on `Show`, launches on SPACE, and materialises the ball back on the paddle whenever it leaves the camera's frame — during a screen change as much as outside one. `MenuOption` (on each arrow) reports a ball hit to it and shatters only if the hit is taken; `CarryOut` then waits out the shatter and either raises `OptionChosen` for `GameManager` (START only), slides `MenuSlider` between the title board and the hall of fame, or advances the hall's champion. First hit wins while `busy`, since the ball is still live throughout — it plays on across the change rather than being re-served after it, passing through both boards until its next bounce (see above).
- `Assets/Scripts/HallOfFame.cs` — the menu's hall of fame screen: the top ten of `RecordBook` by score, one champion at a time, wrapping, scrolled in rather than swapped in (`Advance`, a coroutine the menu waits on) — lines and the "next champion" arrow together, since they are one plaque arriving. Builds the name and score lines at runtime (they are player-typed names, so nothing can be authored) as one hittable block per symbol, and destroys each block with its mesh as it moves on; hides the "next champion" arrow when there is no second champion. Putting knocked-out symbols back is `MainMenuPanel.RestoreBoard`'s business, not this component's — the plaque is mended as part of the board it stands on.
- `Assets/Scripts/BlockText.cs` — the 5 × 7 block font (whole alphabet and digits, `?` for anything else) and the solid geometry built from it: one box per horizontal run of solid cells, UVs offset by the piece's place in a word so masonry runs across separately-built letters. Also `BuildArrowMesh`/`ArrowOutline` for the option arrows, mesh and collider from one outline. In the runtime assembly because `HallOfFame` needs it, and shared from there by the editor setup script.
- `Assets/Scripts/Border.cs` — one of the three walls closing a room at the left, right and top edges of the frame, in the menu and in a round alike. Built and fitted at runtime by the room that owns it — `MainMenuPanel` or `Playfield` — since there is nothing to author: the frame is the window's. Invisible, and the place a ball hit turns into a `Ricochet`. The direction the sparks fly is taken from the wall's own side rather than the contact, so it is the same every time whichever part of the ball touched first. `Border.FrameExtents` is the shared measurement both rooms fit themselves to.
- `Assets/Scripts/Playfield.cs` — the round's room: fits its three borders, the backdrop's size and the paddle's travel to the camera's frame on `OnEnable` and on any window resize, and publishes `HalfWidth`/`HalfHeight` for `GameManager` to lay the brick grid out against.
- `Assets/Scripts/Ricochet.cs` — the burst where a ball strikes a border, on the menu or in a round: a streak lying along the wall that stretches out and thins away, plus a scatter of sparks fanned about the normal. Cosmetic and runtime-only like `Debris`, and moved by hand in `Update` for the same reason — no physics components near the gameplay colliders — but short and small where debris is slow and heavy. Spawned a little way inside the frame, since a burst centred on the screen's edge would spend half of itself outside it, and tinted up off the ball's own material to a pale flame colour. `ClearAll` sweeps the fragments at a screen change, as `Debris.ClearAll` does.
- `Assets/Scripts/MenuOption.cs` — one option arrow; a ball collision is the entire interaction. It shatters into `Debris` and switches itself off only when `MainMenuPanel.OnOptionHit` takes the hit as the choice; a refused hit leaves it standing, because an arrow the player can't get back is a screen they can't leave.
- `Assets/Scripts/MenuTitleBlock.cs` — one block of the menu's lettering, whether a letter of the title or a symbol of the hall of fame's plaque; a ball collision shatters it into `Debris` and switches it off.
- `Assets/Editor/ArkanoidSetup.cs` — `[InitializeOnLoadMethod]` script that generates the sprite textures (`Assets/Sprites`), configures their importers, creates the bouncy `PhysicsMaterial2D` (`Assets/Physics`) and the `Ball`/`Brick` prefabs (`Assets/Prefabs`), then authors the camera, the playfield room, the paddle and a wired `GameManager` into the scene, and finally the `ScoreBoard` screen-space canvas. Editor-authored content stays inspectable without Play mode; only the ball and bricks exist at runtime.

## Editor setup is staged and resumable

`ArkanoidSetup.Setup()` performs **one** stage per domain reload and returns, relying on the next reload to observe the previous stage's output (textures → importers → physics material → prefabs → scene → scoreboard UI → records panel → deferred save → crack texture variants → crack importers → brick-prefab crack overlay → Lit materials → ball prefab to sphere → brick prefab to box → 3D scene retrofit → deferred save → squared wall corners → deferred save → brick wall texture → textured wall materials → wall material swap → deferred save → world-UV wall meshes → wall mesh swap → deferred save → randomized-crack prefab retrofit → rounded paddle mesh → paddle mesh/collider swap → deferred save → centered light position → deferred save → centered light direction → deferred save → rounded brick mesh → brick variant prefabs → GameManager variant wiring → deferred save → menu block meshes → menu materials → menu screen → deferred save → grouped HUD without its high score → deferred save → flattened light tilt → deferred save → menu panel stripped to its options → deferred save → menu screen rebuilt → deferred save → hall of fame moved left of the board → deferred save → menu moved into its own room → deferred save → playfield opened out to the frame → deferred save). Stages 9 and 10 authored the menu as a UGUI panel and are gone now that the menu is scene content; the numbering after them was left alone rather than shuffled. Stages 40 and 50 are the ones the menu keeps coming back to: stage 40 rebuilds the block meshes whenever a new one is missing (which is how the arrows arrived and the title letters were rewritten at their smaller cell, and where the labelled slabs it all replaced were deleted), and stage 50 throws the whole `MenuScreen` away and re-runs stage 42's builder whenever its shape is behind that builder — first when the UGUI option list became a playable screen, then when the screen became two screens on a slider. Both guards are written against the *current* end state, so they stay quiet once the scene matches and are correct on a from-scratch build. Stages 52 and 54 are the exception to that habit: swapping the hall of fame to the board's left, and moving the whole menu into a room of its own left of the playfield (with its backdrop pulled in to that room's width), each moved a transform or two and nothing else, so they edit in place rather than paying for another wholesale rebuild. Stage 56 is the same kind of edit at a larger scale: it deletes the three masonry walls, puts a `Playfield` component on the room they hung off, re-anchors the four HUD texts to the bottom of the canvas and scales the menu's paddle and ball — all scene edits, so they fit in one pass, since only asset writes have to be read back on a later reload. Stages 17–27 still build, texture and re-mesh those walls on a from-scratch run and stage 56 still takes them out afterwards: keeping the intermediate valid is cheaper than unpicking ten stages, and each of their guards already no-ops once the walls are gone. Stage 24's did not — an "asset guid not in the scene yet" gate goes true again the moment the walls that referenced it are deleted — so it now also requires a wall to still be standing. The crack textures are generated from a per-variant seeded `System.Random` walk (`Assets/Sprites/CrackLight0-3.png` / `CrackHeavy0-3.png`), so a from-scratch rebuild reproduces the same set. Every stage that edits the scene only marks it dirty; a paired follow-up stage saves it from a self-removing `EditorApplication.update` handler on the next editor tick, to avoid the modal dialog described below. This works around `AssetDatabase` writes not being reliably readable back via `LoadAssetAtPath` within the same call. If you touch this file, preserve the one-stage-per-reload structure. The scene stage is guarded by `GameObject.Find("GameManager")`, so once set up the whole method is a no-op.

Folder existence is checked on disk (`System.IO.Directory`) rather than via `AssetDatabase.IsValidFolder`, which can go stale in driven sessions.

**Caution:** the scene stage calls `EditorSceneManager.SaveScene` from `[InitializeOnLoadMethod]`; in one session Unity then detected the open scene's file change and raised a *modal* "scene changed on disk" dialog that blocked the Editor main thread (and the Uplink bridge) until dismissed by hand. Either button is safe (disk and memory content are identical), but be aware programmatic scene saves can trigger it.
